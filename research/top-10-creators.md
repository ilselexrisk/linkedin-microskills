# Top 10 LinkedIn creators for B2B lead generation

**Researched:** 2026-11-17
**Ranking method:** relevance to B2B lead generation, not follower count. A follower ranking puts CEOs like Bill Gates and Satya Nadella at the top, and their posting style can't be copied by a normal marketer.

Each profile is summarised in our own words. Follower counts are approximate and come from the cited sources at the time they were published.

| # | Creator | Niche | Signature move | Lead mechanic | Steal this |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Justin Welsh** | Solopreneur, personal brand | Turns every winning post into a reusable template | Newsletter + digital products | Keep a "winners" file and re-run the structure, not the words |
| 2 | **Jasmin Alić** | LinkedIn copywriting | Hook = promise, then a "rehook" in lines 2–3 | Courses, consulting, community from comments | Write the first 3 lines last. Add *signposting*: say how you know what you know |
| 3 | **Lara Acosta** | Personal branding | The SLAY framework: Story → Lesson → Actionable → You | Lead magnets → email list → offers | Short, vulnerable story first, advice second |
| 4 | **Adam Robinson** (RB2B) | Founder-led SaaS | Dense hooks: credibility + real numbers + bold claim + open loop | Posts → free product sign-ups; best posts reused as thought-leader ads | Exact figures (€, days, counts) in the first two lines |
| 5 | **Dave Gerhardt** (Exit Five) | B2B marketing | Personal stories over expert lectures; posts daily | Community memberships | Write "what happened to me + what I took from it", not "5 tips" |
| 6 | **Chris Walker** (Passetto) | Demand generation | Contrarian takes on B2B marketing; video and live sessions | Inbound consulting deals | Create demand (educate) instead of gating content to capture leads |
| 7 | **Chris Donnelly** | Founders, business | Visual-first: mostly image posts and carousels | Newsletter, accelerator, agency | One idea per visual; cheat-sheet format |
| 8 | **Daniel Murray** (Marketing Millennials) | Marketing education | Comment-first growth; screenshot/meme posts | Agency (Authority B2B) + sponsorships | Treat comments on big creators' posts as "mini-posts" |
| 9 | **Richard van der Blom** | LinkedIn algorithm data | Yearly Algorithm Insights report (1M+ posts) | Social-selling training for companies | Base decisions on data: comments and saves carry the most weight; links get less reach |
| 10 | **Ruben Hassid** | AI education | One topic, one format, ~6 posts/week; covers news fast | Free guide → email list | Pick a narrow lane and become "the person for X" |

## Patterns that repeat across all 10

1. **The hook decides everything.** Readers see only the first ~140 characters before "…see more". Most creators write the hook last.
2. **Story beats advice.** Gerhardt, Acosta and Robinson all say personal stories outperform generic expert tips. Data from Ordinal (309K posts) also shows story hooks beating question hooks.
3. **Specificity = credibility.** Real numbers, names, timeframes.
4. **One idea per post.** Alić asks "what is the ONE thing the reader should learn?" and puts that in the hook.
5. **Comments are distribution.** Van der Blom: comments are the strongest ranking signal, and author replies after the first hour push the post back into feeds. Murray grows mainly by commenting on others' posts.
6. **Consistency compounds.** Gerhardt: daily for years. Acosta: about 5 a week while growing. Most people can sustain 3 a week.
7. **Leads come from a clear next step, not from going viral.** Welsh, Acosta and Hassid all route readers to an email list or a free resource. Robinson routes them to a free product.

## Where they disagree (trade-offs for us)

- **Comment-gating** ("comment X and I'll DM you"): Donnelly-style posts use engagement gates; Robinson advises skipping these gimmicks. → Our skills offer a soft CTA by default and a gated one only as an option.
- **Lead gen vs demand gen:** Walker argues that grabbing contact details early hits buyers before they're ready. → Our skills aim for trust first and give one low-friction next step.
- **Format:** Alić defends text-only; Donnelly is image- and carousel-first; Hassid uses no carousels at all. → Start with text, which is cheapest to test, and add visuals later.

## Swedish market note

Swedish B2B audiences tend to reward understatement (*lagom*). One-line "broetry", hype words and dramatic "I was broke → now I'm rich" arcs come across as American and lose trust. The Swedish skills adjust for this.
